Summary

Kelmer Neves is the founder and CEO of Nationwide Immigration LLC. Born on September 7, 1976, in Cabo Frio, Rio de Janeiro, he immigrated to the United States in 1999. After receiving a removal order, he studied immigration law and fought his case for 15 years. The case, Neves v. Holder, reached the U.S. Supreme Court, which in 2010 vacated the lower court's ruling and remanded it in light of Kucana v. Holder. On remand, the First Circuit held that federal courts may review the Board of Immigration Appeals' denial of equitable tolling in motions to reopen (Neves v. Holder, 613 F.3d 30, 1st Cir. 2010). He obtained permanent residence (a Green Card) in 2014 and became a U.S. citizen in 2019. Today he helps immigrants in the United States. He holds a Bachelor of Science (cum laude, 2016) from New York University's School of Professional Studies, an Associate of Science in Paralegal Studies (2005) from Ashworth College, and a certificate in Immigration Law (2003) from the Washington Online Learning Institute. He speaks English, Portuguese and Spanish. His story is the subject of the documentary "Kelmer Neves: Impossible Is an Opinion" (2020), directed by Tiago Lopes, and of the book "Impossível é uma opinião" (Autografia, in Portuguese, December 2022).

Kelmer was born in Cabo Frio, in the state of Rio de Janeiro. In 1999, he moved to the United States and settled in South Yarmouth, on Cape Cod, Massachusetts. He studied paralegal studies at Ashworth College, where he earned an Associate of Science in Paralegal Studies in 2005, and immigration law at the Washington Online Learning Institute, where he earned a certificate in 2003. He graduated with a Bachelor of Science in International Business and Global Management from New York University's School of Professional Studies, cum laude (an academic distinction awarded for outstanding performance), in 2016.

The Neves v. Holder case

Summary. Kelmer Neves spent 15 years in a legal battle that reached the United States Supreme Court. His case, Neves v. Holder (1st Cir. 2010), addressed when federal courts can review denials of motions to reopen deportation proceedings.

  1. 1999–2001

    Arrival and the immigration case

    Kelmer entered the United States in 1999 as a B-2 visitor and overstayed. On December 2, 1999, the Immigration and Naturalization Service (INS) began removal proceedings against him. Kelmer conceded removability and applied for asylum and withholding of removal. At this stage he was represented by a person who held himself out as an attorney but was not licensed to practice law. According to the court's opinion, that person fled a criminal prosecution in Massachusetts. On November 1, 2000, the immigration judge denied asylum and withholding of removal and found Kelmer ineligible for voluntary departure.

  2. 2001–2002

    The dismissed appeal

    The unlicensed representative filed an appeal to the Board of Immigration Appeals (BIA) but listed as the mailing address a post office box he stopped checking when he fled. In June 2001, Kelmer hired another attorney, who did not enter an appearance until April 2002. Unable to reach Kelmer, the BIA concluded the appeal had been abandoned and dismissed it on September 25, 2001. Kelmer learned of the dismissal in August 2002.

  3. 2003

    First motion to reopen

    On August 21, 2003, with a different attorney, Kelmer asked the BIA to reopen the case. The motion was already untimely, and he blamed the delay on ineffective assistance by his previous representatives. On December 3, 2003, the BIA denied it as untimely, rejected equitable tolling and found he had not shown due diligence. That decision was not timely challenged in court.

  4. 2006

    Second motion to reopen

    On June 30, 2006, with a new attorney, Kelmer filed a second motion to reopen, this time seeking adjustment of status under section 245(i) of the U.S. immigration law. He argued that the deficient handling of the first motion justified equitable tolling. On December 14, 2006, the BIA denied the motion as barred by time and number limits and found that Kelmer had not shown due diligence, since he could not give dates or details of his alleged contacts with the previous attorney. The BIA also declined to reopen the case on its own authority (sua sponte). Kelmer petitioned the U.S. Court of Appeals for the First Circuit.

  5. 2009

    The First Circuit finds it lacks jurisdiction

    On June 4, 2009, the First Circuit denied the petition, holding that it lacked jurisdiction to review the BIA's due diligence finding or its refusal to reopen sua sponte (Da Silva Neves v. Holder, 568 F.3d 41).

  6. 2010

    The Supreme Court

    Kelmer petitioned the Supreme Court (No. 09-650). While the petition was pending, the Court decided Kucana v. Holder (2010). The government then dropped its jurisdictional position and recommended that the Court grant the petition, vacate and remand. On May 17, 2010, the Supreme Court granted certiorari, vacated the First Circuit's judgment and remanded the case for further consideration in light of Kucana v. Holder.

  7. July 21, 2010

    The First Circuit's decision

    On remand, the First Circuit (a per curiam decision by Judges Lynch, Torruella and Ebel) held that it has jurisdiction to review the BIA's denial of equitable tolling, but not the BIA's refusal to reopen sua sponte. On the merits, it held that even assuming equitable tolling was available, the BIA did not abuse its discretion in denying it, because substantial evidence supported the finding that Kelmer had not shown due diligence. The petition was denied.

What the case means. The decision confirms, in the First Circuit, that federal courts may review the BIA's denial of equitable tolling in motions to reopen. It is a ruling of that court (which covers Massachusetts, Maine, New Hampshire, Rhode Island and Puerto Rico), not of the whole country. Kelmer's own petition was denied on the merits.

After 2010. In 2014, after fifteen years of litigation, Kelmer obtained permanent residence (a Green Card), and in 2019 he became a U.S. citizen.

Career

Kelmer is the founder and CEO of Nationwide Immigration LLC, an immigration company headquartered in Washington, D.C. In 2025, he gave a series of talks at chapters of the Brazilian Bar Association (OAB) in the state of Rio de Janeiro on immigration and on the procedures for Brazilian lawyers to obtain authorization to practice law in the United States.

Documentary

Kelmer's story is told in the documentary Kelmer Neves: Impossible Is an Opinion, directed by Tiago Lopes. Released in November 2020 in Brazil and the United States, the film features testimonies from people connected to the case, including one of the lawyers who worked on it. The release was the subject of a Globo Internacional program. Kelmer had already been interviewed by the Globo news program Globo Notícia Américas.
